# Commercial licensing

The FRED documentation and the code that renders it are available under the
[PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0](LICENSE).
That licence covers research, teaching, study and any other noncommercial
purpose. It does **not** grant commercial use.

If you want to use FRED commercially, that is a separate licence and it is
available. Email **contact@biofred.us**.

## What counts as commercial

The licence text is authoritative, but as a rough guide, you need a commercial
licence if you intend to:

- use FRED, or any part of it, in work you are paid for — consulting studies,
  siting analyses, feasibility reports delivered to a client;
- run it as, or inside, a product or service you charge for;
- use its outputs in a commercial procurement or investment decision;
- embed the models or the supply-curve logic in proprietary software.

You do **not** need one to:

- run it for research, coursework, or a thesis;
- use it at a university, national lab, public research organisation,
  government body, or environmental non-profit — this holds regardless of how
  that work is funded, including industry-funded research;
- study the code, modify it, and publish what you found;
- cite it, benchmark against it, or reproduce its results.

If your case sits on the boundary, ask. A short email is cheaper for both of us
than a wrong assumption.

## What a commercial licence covers

Terms depend on the use, but a licence would ordinarily cover the source code
in this repository. The **trained model artefacts** — the harvest cost
surrogate and the feasibility classifier — are separate works, not licensed by
this repository at all (see [NOTICE](NOTICE)), and are negotiated separately.
For most commercial uses those weights are the part that actually matters, so
say up front whether you need them.

Note also that a licence to this code is not a licence to the underlying input
data. FRCS output, USDA FSim layers and third-party geospatial datasets carry
their providers' terms, which we cannot sublicense.

## Academic collaboration

If you are at a university or a public research organisation, you already have
what you need under the noncommercial licence — no email required. Do get in
touch anyway if you are building on the method, want the model weights, or are
considering a joint paper. That is more interesting than a licence.
